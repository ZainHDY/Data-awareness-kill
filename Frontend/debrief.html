Thank you — here's the full picture

This study wasn't really about "browsing a social feed." It was studying whether people notice and understand data collection that isn't clearly disclosed up front — especially in places where data-protection rules are still developing.

To measure that honestly, parts of what was actually happening had to stay unstated until now. That's a recognized research method called a deception design, and it's why you saw the report on the previous page before this explanation.

What wasn't disclosed upfront, and why

  • The cookie choice mismatch: clicking "Reject all" didn't necessarily block the analytics cookie. This tests whether people notice when a stated choice isn't actually honored — a real pattern seen on production websites.
  • Passive fingerprinting and behavioral tracking: device details, scroll depth, per-post dwell time, and comment-field interactions were all recorded in the background during the "feed" step, without a banner calling each one out individually.
  • The ad-targeting demo: the simplified ad example showed how easily browsing behavior maps to targeting — this is illustrative, not a real ad network, and nothing was actually sold or shared.

Nothing collected here was sold, shared with third parties, or used for anything beyond this research.

Protecting yourself going forward

  • Browser cookie/tracking settings and extensions that block third-party trackers reduce (but don't eliminate) this kind of tracking.
  • Fingerprinting resistant browser modes exist in some browsers and can make sessions harder to link together.
  • Reading a site's cookie banner carefully and checking whether "reject" is actually respected is a habit worth having — as you just saw, it isn't always honored.
